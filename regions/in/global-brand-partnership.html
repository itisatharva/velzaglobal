GLOBAL BRAND PARTNERSHIP: HOW BRANDS ENTER NEW MARKETS

Introduction

A global brand faces the same dilemma shortly after entering a new market. Developing the right global market entry strategy becomes crucial when entering a completely unfamiliar market with a limited initial presence and opportunities. Markets may seem simple in a presentation on a slide, but choosing the right market for entry is not a frequent occurrence for most companies. As a result, brands often lack experience and may overlook essential elements that require local market knowledge.

Executives realize that choosing the wrong formula for entering the market will have short-term consequences for sales growth. However, it will damage the most valuable asset of the brand, its desirability, which is much more challenging to restore. At the same time, the correct positioning and formula create ideal momentum for the next stages of development. Therefore, we will discuss how brands can enter unfamiliar markets and what attention is paid to during this process.

Why Local Infrastructure Often Matters More Than a Global Name

Premium brands are often surprised by the amount of local infrastructure that needs to be involved to generate sales. Distributors must be identified, and retail chains must receive goods for resale. Of course, it is necessary to create demand for goods, which is a long and resource-intensive process. However, most of these activities can be delegated to local partners, as some companies have already mastered the skills and tools for this work. This is the essence of strategic partnerships, which allow a brand to use the knowledge of local market participants.

A brand that wants to sell its premium spirit drinks has no choice but to turn to local distributors for assistance. Not only does their responsibility extend to securing cold storage, but they also need to find experienced local supermarkets, hypermarkets, and HORECA retailers. Finally, the partner must have extensive knowledge of local legislation, brand development, and the local market in general.

Heineken's entry into the Philippine market is a prime example of the strategic partnership model. By joining forces with Asia Brewery Incorporated, Heineken was able to establish a joint venture in the country. Heineken's management realized that they would have to wait before building their own sales infrastructure and developing the local market. Therefore, Heineken opted for a joint venture with a local partner with brewing know-how and a sales base, including on-premise outlets. The same approach is used by many international beverage companies when entering emerging markets.

Essential Elements of a Global Brand Partnership

Some strategic partnerships provide only trademarks to local partners, while others transfer their know-how, technology, and management to another company. However, despite the apparent differences, most global brand strategic partnerships have several success factors, although their importance varies.

First, brand philosophy compatibility is paramount, which is nearly impossible to change after the establishment of a partnership. Luxury brands differ significantly from simple retailers, and a local partner who cannot understand this at the stage of partnership establishment will inevitably fail. Second, category knowledge is vital, whether it is cold chain stocking know-how, barista training, or mixology skills for a brand of spirit drinks. The overestimated level of distribution and the partner's ability to balance brand and commercial expertise are also critical. The final factor is the loyalty of the strategic partner, which often proves to be more critical than the previous four. A partnership that does not unravel under pressure possesses many positive qualities.

How Global Brands Enter New Markets, The Process

A strategic partnership usually consists of five steps in bringing a brand to market.

  1. The brand needs to identify and evaluate the characteristics and problems of the local market.
  2. The brand needs to find a potential strategic partner.
  3. The brand needs to be adapted to the local market through a series of transformations.
  4. The brand needs to be launched on the local market through distribution channels controlled by the strategic partner.
  5. The management of the strategic partnership and its control over business processes begin.

Thus, adapting the brand to the local market always implies making concessions to meet the tastes and needs of local consumers. These concessions may include changes to packaging, localization of language, and even adjustments to pricing policies. At the same time, the brand's core components, which constitute its unique selling proposition, must be preserved.

Strategic Partnerships in Coffee Shops, Case Study

Coffee shops resort to strategic partnerships for several reasons. For example, in India, most consumers prefer instant coffee, and there is a certain amount of time for them to get used to a new product. Local products are tailored to local preferences, whereas global ones must adapt to these preferences. Finally, creating a customer base from scratch is a time and resource-intensive process.

Starbucks is the most recognizable coffee shop brand, which initially entered the Indian market under a strategic partnership model. The brand uses the 50 50 joint venture with Tata Consumer Products known as Tata Starbucks. Thus, India has 500 Starbucks Coffee stores as of 2026, and Tata Starbucks expects the number to grow.

In India, another coffee shop brand, Costa Coffee, also uses the strategic partnership model. However, unlike Starbucks, this global company has a negligible presence in the Indian market. The reason for this is the use of a licensing strategy. The headquarters in the UK have identified a strategic partner, Devyani International, which owns the exclusive rights to open Costa Coffee restaurants in India. As of 2023, the number of coffee shops franchised to Devyani International exceeded 200. With the right strategic partner, global coffee shop brands can build consumer loyalty and position themselves in local markets.

Beverage Category, Strategic Partnership Is Even More Critical

There are several reasons why beverage manufacturers rely on strategic partnerships when entering new markets. First, cold chain logistics are essential to moving and storing most beverage products. Second, local legislation and licensing are highly relevant to the beverage category. Third, on-premise sales are vital to building brand image for most beverage products. Finally, consumer education is essential, given the lack of familiarity with most beverage categories and the time it takes to build consumer loyalty.

A strategic partner with extensive local market knowledge will be able to ensure the uninterrupted supply of a beverage to the market. In addition, such a partner will have the expertise and skills to navigate the intricate local licensing and on-premise sales regulations. Moreover, beverage manufacturers will benefit from the local partner's consumer education know-how and experience in implementing sales concepts. Therefore, beverage manufacturers rely on strategic partnerships when entering new markets, in particular Asian and Indian ones.

Critical Success Factors in Strategic Partnership for Brands

When looking for strategic partners, brands should ask themselves several vital questions.

  • How much control does the brand retain over the strategic partner at all stages?
  • How will the termination of the partnership, whether by mutual decision or by the decision of one party, be regulated?
  • Does the strategic partner share the brand's values, or will this be a problem at the stage of partnership launch after exhaustive joint meetings?
  • Is the strategic partner's financial performance transparent and subject to independent audits, or does the brand have to rely solely on the partner's statements?
  • Is the strategic partner ready to scale at the same rate as the brand, or does the brand risk being held back by the partner's capabilities and looking for a new one?

These questions are not always on the minds of brands searching for strategic partners, but most of them face at least some of these challenges.

Trends in Strategic Partnerships

There are several trends in strategic partnerships that have taken shape over the past few years. Brands are witnessing a growing demand for premiumization, and companies are looking for strategic partners with extensive premium retail experience. The importance of omnichannel distribution has grown, and strategic partners must have the capability to operate in the physical retail and digital sales chains. Sustainable development goals (ESG) have become increasingly important, and brands are carefully considering the ESG performance of potential strategic partners. Local production is a critical differentiator, and strategic partners have a distinct advantage over international ones in leveraging this trend.

Conclusion, Finding the Right Place for a Global Brand

Finding the right market for a global brand to enter is only the beginning of the long and strenuous process of bringing the brand to this market. Strategic partnership is the preferred choice for most global brands, as it allows them to build on the strengths of the local partner, including their knowledge and specific skills, without damaging the brand's reputation and image. However, finding the right strategic partner is never easy, and brands must consider all the critical success factors when choosing a partner. Thus, we have discussed the most critical aspects to pay attention to when looking for a strategic partner.
